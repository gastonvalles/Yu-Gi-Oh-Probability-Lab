Privacy and legal notice

Last updated: November 9, 2026

In short

YGOUltraLab is a free tool that runs in your browser. It has no accounts, uses no cookies, includes no analytics or advertising and collects no personal data. What you build stays on your device. The exceptions are the videos in the Replays tab, which YouTube serves, and a bug report if you choose to send one, which is published (see below).

What is stored on your device

The app uses your browser's local storage to remember your work: the deck you're editing, saved decks, practice mat combos, your settings (language, format, mascot, floating button positions), the LP calculator, recent searches and copies of card results so they load faster. None of this is sent to a server of ours. You can delete it whenever you want from the site data in your browser settings. To open without a connection, the app also keeps a copy of its own code and images on your device: no personal data; it is also cleared with site data.

Who the site talks to

  • Vercel, where it is hosted: receives the technical data of any web visit, such as the IP address and browser, to deliver the pages.
  • YGOPRODeck (db.ygoprodeck.com): your browser asks it directly for card data. That's why YGOPRODeck sees those requests, including what you search for, and your IP address. Card images, on the other hand, are served by this site: it requests them from images.ygoprodeck.com once and keeps them in its cache, so YGOPRODeck doesn't see which cards you look at (only if that copy fails does your browser request the image directly).
  • YouTube (Google): in the Replays tab, video thumbnails are requested from i.ytimg.com as soon as they appear on screen. The player (youtube-nocookie.com) isn't loaded until you tap Play: that's when YouTube sees your IP address and may store its own cookies.
  • Project Ignis (GitHub), in the automatic duel: your browser asks only this site for the card data and their scripts; this site takes them from Project Ignis's public repository when it's published, so GitHub doesn't see which cards you use. The rules engine runs in your browser.
  • External sites such as LinkedIn, Instagram, GitHub or Konami's database: when you open their links, their own policies apply.

If you report a bug

The report is published as a public issue in the site's GitHub repository, visible to anyone: the area, what you wrote and, only if you left it checked, your browser and operating system family, the language and the site version. It asks for no account, email or name: don't write personal data in the text. The site's server sends it, so GitHub doesn't see your IP address; the site uses it only in memory, for up to a day, to stop spam, and neither stores nor publishes it.

A security vulnerability isn't reported there, because it's public: follow SECURITY.md.

Your data and contact

Apart from the bug reports you send, the site doesn't store personal data on servers of its own: there is no information about you to look up, correct or delete here, it's in your browser and you control it. If you want a report of yours deleted, or for any question, write to Gastón Valles on LinkedIn or Instagram.
